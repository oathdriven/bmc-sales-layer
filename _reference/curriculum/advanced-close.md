## 0. What it is

The standard close is 1 line:

> *"It costs $X,XXX.XX to get started. What do you want to do next?"*

The advanced close **reframes the close before it arrives**. Instead of landing
the number cold, you walk them through a sequence that collects agreement, then
you flip the price to a better one on a transition word.

> **"You always say the bad thing first BUT the good thing second."**

It covers a lot of objections inside the reframing, before they are ever spoken.

---

## 1. The permission ask (first yes)

**Before anything is on screen**, you ask permission to show them the offer.

That is the **first yes**.

Note the order. Permission comes FIRST, the screen comes second. You are not
asking them to react to a price, you are asking for the right to show them
anything at all, which is the easiest yes in the whole call.

---

## 2. The screen lands

Now the offer goes up, and they see the price.

**They do not know yet that this price is conditional.**

---

## 3. The guarantee ask (second yes)

Now you ask if it is ok to explain the guarantee.

That is the **second yes**.

> ⚠️ Both yeses are PERMISSION asks, not agreements. Nobody has agreed to
> anything about the offer yet. They have twice agreed to let you keep talking,
> which is why both are easy to give.

---

## 4. The guarantee, and the frame landing

**The screen sits there the entire time you explain the guarantee.**

> **While explaining the guarantee, say NOTHING about the price.**

This is the part that looks like nothing and does the most work. You are talking
about the guarantee, so their attention is on your voice, **and the everyday
price is landing the whole time in their peripheral vision.** By the time you
finish, that number has been sitting in front of them for a minute or more and
has stopped being a surprise.

Ryan, 2026-07-28: *"the screen sits while you do this so the frame of the every
day price lands."*

That is why the reveal later reads as relief rather than as a discount pitch.
The first number is already installed.

---

## 5. The value stack

Run down everything they are going to get, **line by line.**

- Total value adds to **$15,000**
- Beneath the line-item pricing sits the real price: **$7,500 first month plus
  $2,500 a month**
- **The stack is always worth more than the price.** Value stacked above the
  number, price underneath it.

---

## 6. The BUT (the whole trick)

**Before you let them object, rebut, or before you ask another question,** you
go:

> **"BUT"**

Then straight to the next slide.

> *"For everyone that makes the decision with me on this call, they get
> rewarded, because everyone knows money loves speed."*

---

## 7. The reveal slide

The next slide shows **everything from the last page**, identical, except the
price:

- **$5,000 to get started**
- **only $2,000 a month**

The price they thought it was becomes a better price, on the transition word.

---

## 8. The landing (unchanged)

It ends exactly where the standard close ends:

> **"So what do you wanna do next?"**

Then silence. Same rule as always: *"I mean it, DO NOT SAY ANOTHER WORD."*

**This is the point that keeps the advanced close inside the framework instead
of beside it.** It is not a different close. It is a longer runway into the same
landing. Everything in sections 1 to 7 exists to make that question easy to
answer, and the question itself never changes.

---

## Why it works

- The bad number is said first, so the good number lands as relief instead of
  as a pitch.
- 2 yeses are already collected before the number is real.
- The guarantee is agreed to while the price is visible but unspoken, which
  defuses it.
- The value stack means the number is compared to $15,000, not to zero.
- **The reframing covers a lot of objections before they are spoken**, which is
  the point: you are not handling objections, you are pre-empting them.
- The discount is not a discount. It is a **reward for deciding on the call**,
  which is the same speed doctrine as the rest of the framework.
- And it still ends in a question, which is the oldest rule in the whole
  system: *always end in a question, never end in a sentence.*

### The 2 objections it kills before they are spoken (Ryan, 2026-07-28)

**"Follow up with me in 2 weeks."** Normally this is the most common polite lie
in sales, and you need the spade to get under it. After this close it barely
survives being said out loud:

> *"Follow up in 2 weeks is an even obviouser lie they said no, because it's a
> nice discount to act now."*

And the math makes the lie visible (Ryan, 2026-08-07, verbatim):

> *"No one says follow up with me in 2 weeks and I'll just pay $8,500 more for
> the year."*

The arithmetic, from the worked example below: waiting costs $2,500 more to
start plus $500 more a month, which is $8,500 over the year. Nobody chooses
that on purpose, so "2 weeks" can only mean no.

You have just given them a real, honest reason to decide today. If they still
want 2 weeks, both people in the conversation can see that the delay is not
about timing. **The close does not beat the objection, it makes the objection
transparent**, which means the spade lands with far less force behind it.

**The guarantee objection.** Gone, because you got ahead of it. The guarantee is
explained and AGREED TO in section 4, before the price is ever spoken. It cannot
come back later as a reason not to buy, because they already told you it made
sense.

That is the whole logic of the advanced close: it is not a better pitch, it is
the same 3 handlers moved EARLIER, into a place where they cost nothing.

## Guardrails (Ryan's own framing, do not lose these)

1. **This is ADVANCED.** The short close is still the default and still what
   gets taught first. Do not put this in front of somebody who has not run the
   7 stages clean.
2. **The conditional price must be real. CONFIRMED REAL** (Ryan, 2026-07-28):
   > *"Closers don't lie to prospects. It is the condition. And some people did
   > come back and had to pay the price. The ones who 1-call closed got the
   > discount. That's how this works."*

   People who came back later genuinely paid $7,500 + $2,500. That is what makes
   this a reward and not fake scarcity, and it is the difference between this
   tactic and the guru version of it. **If you are not willing to hold the line
   when somebody comes back next week, you do not get to use this close.**
   Say the bad number first only if the bad number is real.
3. **The stack must be real.** Every line item is something they actually get.
