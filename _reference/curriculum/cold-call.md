## 0. The one rule everything hangs on

> **The job is to close on a future appointment, not to close on a deal.**

You have earned **1 more phone call.** Not a demo. Not a sale. The 15 minutes.

---

## 1. The gatekeeper

**The goal: sound like a friend of the owner, not a vendor.**

- Ask for the business owner **by first name**, then **shut up.**
- If asked who's calling, give **your first name only**, as if you know the owner, then
  **shut up.**
- **Stay away from last names. Their first, and your first.**

> WRONG: *"This is RyMac from ABC Agency, is Phil Collins around?"*
> RIGHT: *"Hey Dora! Is Phil available?"*

**If she asks who's calling:**
> *"You can let him know it's RyMac."*

**If she asks what it's about**, name the problem or use real research on that specific
company:
> *"Yeah sure, it's about his [problem] — how quickly he's getting back to his
> customers / it's about a bad review and I had a question / it's about no one calling
> me back / someone referred me to Phil / there's an issue on his website"*

Whatever the case is, **don't pitch her.** Be **vaguely confident without making up a
bald face lie.**

> **You don't use your USP or help statement on a gatekeeper.** Their job is to sniff
> you out. Your job is to act like you're calling a friend, and they will patch you
> right through.

---

## 2. The owner: the opener

> *"Hey Phil, it's Ryan, how are you doing today?"*

**Carry the same confidence as if they know you. This buys you precious seconds.**

They'll answer *"I'm doing good, how are you? How can I help you? What's this about?"*

### The core line

> *"I'm great! The reason that I called is because [I help X businesses fix Y thing in
> Z time]. Is this worth discussing further when you have the time?"*

### If you have results or testimonials in the industry already

> *"I'm great! The reason that I called is [1-sentence case study]. Recently we helped
> Dr. X double his weekly amount of new patients by reactivating his database. Is this
> worth discussing further when you have time?"*

### Protip: if you have any real intel

If you're already a customer, saw they did something cool, or have any intel on the
business, use it:

> *"I'm great, and I'm actually a customer / noticed you have 100 5-star reviews /
> [saw you] released a new product line, love your stuff! The reason I called is
> [boom]"*

### The two fixed parts

1. **Always say "the reason that I called is:"** then state your reason.
2. **Then ask if it's worth discussing further.**

### The redirect that books it (captured 2026-08-07, Ryan's words)

Just like a sales call, never get into the weeds. If they ask what it is or how you do it,
say you'd love to send them something [roadmap / 1-pager] and discuss it on the call.
**Assume the call and redirect:**

> *"I'd love to send you something to look at and discuss it on the call. Is tomorrow in
> the morning at 9, or closer to 1 in the afternoon, good for a 15 minute call?"*

The ask is an alternative choice between 2 times, never a yes/no.

**And the why, verbatim:**

> *"I know I say the job is not to say AI or get in the weeds, but to be clear: because many
> people will get nervous, talk themselves out of the next call. You're not going to close
> someone on a cold call. Redirect and get the call scheduled."*

The weeds law (section 5) says what not to do. This is what you DO instead, and the nerves
are the reason it has to be a reflex: the redirect exists so the caller cannot talk
themselves out of the booking.

---

## 3. Booking it

Grab a **time** and their **email**, then:

> *"I'll call you at this time, let me send you an email with a
> 1-pager / roadmap / something to look at so our upcoming call makes sense."*

**You're setting a 15 minute phone call here, not a full on sales demo.** You've only
earned 1 more phone call, not a sales call that scares them.

---

## 4. The 15-minute call: earning the real one

**Your job on the 15 minute call is to earn the 30-60 minute call** (or however long,
and whatever medium, you need to run your framework).

During those 15 minutes you *can* transition into the full sales framework if it's
there and you did steps 1-2 correctly. But **if you're pivoting, get a commitment.**

### The advanced move (do NOT try this on your first few appointments)

If you know you can help them and you have **diagnosed their pain**, and they're asking
really good questions that set the alarm off in your head:

> *"You know something Phil? I can definitely help you get to [desired outcome] — do
> you have another 25-35 minutes and are you near a computer? I can show you everything
> we just talked about so that it makes sense."*

### Otherwise, the standard book-forward

> *"Great! I know I can help you get to [dream outcome] — do you have 25-35 minutes
> this afternoon, or tomorrow morning, to jump on a call so I can show you the thing
> and make sure you see the clear path?"*

**Then you close a demo, then you close on the demo.**

---

## 5. Never say "AI"

> *"I've been selling AI for 5 years. Notice not once anywhere did I say AI, or anything
> besides what lies in the framework that pushes people down the customer journey."*

Corroborated live by a design partner's own dials: the trades reply **"what's even
AI."** They are not skeptical of it. They are **illiterate** about it. You cannot hook
someone in 5 seconds on a category they can't define.

> **CONFIRMED AND WIDENED 2026-07-31.** "Never say AI" is the most common instance of a
> bigger law, in Ryan's words:
>
> ### The weeds law
>
> > **"As soon as they get into the weeds of their offer on a cold call, they've lost. All
> > they have done is created more questions and objections from a stranger that wasn't
> > looking to be sold."**
>
> **The objective of a cold call is to gauge interest about FIXING THE PROBLEM.** That is what
> earns the right to a 15 minute phone call, and the 15 minute call is where everything gets
> explained. You are not selling the solution. You are finding out if the problem is worth 15
> minutes to them.
>
> **Volunteering technology or offer detail is the failure.** Saying "AI" is one form of it.
> Explaining how it works, what the stack is, what the deliverable includes, all the same
> failure. Every sentence of detail manufactures a question and an objection from someone who
> never asked to be sold.
>
> ### The recovery, when THEY ask about the technology
>
> They will ask. Being asked is not the failure. Answering in the weeds is.
>
> > *"That's a great question, and that's exactly why I wanted to set up a future call and
> > send over something to look at."*
>
> **Then redirect into a question** that moves toward booking. Do not stop on the deflection,
> do not wait, do not let the silence invite a follow-up question. Answer, bridge, ask.
>
> **Teach this as a reflex, not a rule.** It is the single most common place a beginner loses
> a call they had already won.

## 6. The awareness ladder runs BACKWARDS on a cold call

**~1% of the people you cold call will be most aware.** (Ryan has closed deals off cold
calls, but *"it's rare and I'm good."*)

> **CONFIRMED 2026-07-31.** Ryan: *"the 1 percent comes from data, the customer journey."*
> The figure is the standard **awareness-ladder distribution**, not Ryan's own dial data and
> not an invented number. It is quotable as-is. Attribute it to the customer journey, never
> to "our calls."

On a cold call, assume **unaware**, because they are:

- he or she was **unaware you existed**
- they may have been **unaware they had [X problem]**
- they may have been **solution aware** already
- possibly **product aware**

> **Cold call: you start at UNAWARE and walk them down the ladder to MOST AWARE.**
> **Inbound (a form fill, an ad click): you start at the other end and walk back the
> other way.**

This is where the 3 Laws get broken down, and it is why outbound messaging and inbound
messaging are not the same message.

## Do / Don't (quick reference)

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| First names only, yours and theirs | Use last names |
| Shut up after you ask | Fill the silence |
| "The reason that I called is…" | Open with a pitch |
| Ask "is this worth discussing further?" | Ask for the sale |
| Be vaguely confident with the gatekeeper | Pitch the gatekeeper |
| Use real intel if you have it | Make up a bald face lie |
| Book 15 minutes | Book a demo |
| Send a 1-pager before the call | Show up cold to your own call |
| Assume they're unaware | Assume they know what you do |
| **Hang up and go to the next one** | Say the word "AI" |
| **Gauge interest in fixing the PROBLEM** | Explain the solution |
| Deflect a tech question, then ask one back | Answer a tech question in the weeds |

**The named failure mode**, from a design partner's own first week:
> *"I was nervous at first. **Rushing to get a pitch out.**"*
