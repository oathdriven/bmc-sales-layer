# The journey: 3 stages and the gate

## The stages

**BUILD is bigger than the offer.** Ryan: *"You build first: offer creation, sales page,
funnels etc, then you have something to market."* You cannot point traffic at nothing, which
is why page building, headlines, exit pops, call funnels, and follow-up sequences all belong
to BUILD, not MARKET.

| Stage | Folder | They arrive with | They leave with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BUILD** | `01_offer` | an idea, a skill, or nothing | an offer, priced, with a floor they can defend, **and the thing they point people at** |
| **MARKET** | `02_gameplan` | something to sell, nobody knows it exists | 1 lane and a daily number |
| **CLOSE** | `03_reps` | a calendar and no confidence | reps, a scorecard, and a $/call number |

## The lane sits at the SEAM, and it decides how much you build

The lane choice is the last question of BUILD and the first question of MARKET, because
**the lane determines what has to exist before you can start.** Nobody builds something they
do not need yet, which is the anti-overwhelm principle applied structurally.

| Lane | Must be built first | Can wait |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold calling** | Offer, price, roadmap, profit calculator | The page, the capture machine, the sequence |
| **Cold email** | Offer, price, a destination, the follow-up sequence | The full page, ads infrastructure |
| **Paid ads** | **Everything.** Page, letter, exit pop, capture, follow-up | Nothing. The most expensive lane to enter. |

**And the lane is a router, not a preference.** Picking it fires a specific stack of tools.
Paid ads fires the landing-page stack. Cold email fires the outbound machine. Cold calling
fires the lead scraper and the confirmed cold-call doctrine.

## Assume square one, always

Ryan: *"We can only assume they don't have an AI and just see the buzz and want to build
market and close on offers."*

The default user has nothing, not even an AI. Everyone further along gets **fast-forwarded by
their own answers**, never by being asked to self-assess. And every step carries the teaching
for why it is built that way, so they are **training and building at the same time.**

**Folders are named for the artifact, not the verb.** `01_offer` not `01_build`. See hard
rule 1 in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Module 2 has TWO tracks, and that is the market gap

**This is the thing competitors do not do.** Ryan's research finding:

> **"One of the things that came back in research was no clear plan. People were just teaching
> aggressive closing stuff."**

Everyone sells tactics. Nobody hands you a plan. So module 2 does not teach outreach in the
abstract, it produces **1 of 2 concrete plans**, picked by what fits the person:

| Track | What it is | Cost to them |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold call** | Ryan's framework. X dials a day, this script, goal is the appointment | time only |
| **Cold email** | The machine Ryan runs his own outbound on: sending tool + lead database + verification | paid tools |

**Both are taught by Ryan and both are real.** The cold email track uses paid tooling Ryan is
an affiliate of, because it is literally the stack he runs his own outbound on. That is not a
conflict, it is the honest version: he recommends the thing he uses, and says so.

**The user picks a track, they do not get assigned one.** An extrovert who hates writing picks
calls. A builder who hates the phone picks email. Both get a plan with real numbers.

## The gate: RESOLVED. There is no gate.

The worry: a beginner jumping straight to practicing closing with nothing to close on.

### The answer: make the whole journey VISIBLE, and nobody needs gating

Ryan, and this is the resolution:

> "It should feel almost like they're learning, and **where they are is what they click into**
> to pick up their particular journey. Anyone who lands on it can **see where they fit into
> it**. If it just shows calls, it gets the people who still need an offer to leave. But if
> they see the offer creation plus plan, they know they can plug in."

**A gate solves the wrong problem.** The beginner is not going to skip ahead if the screen
shows them, plainly, that step 1 is building an offer and they have not done it. **They
self-locate.**

And the failure the gate would have caused is the opposite of the one it was designed to
prevent: **an app that opens on "practice calls" tells the guy with no offer that this
product is not for him, and he leaves.** Showing the whole journey is what retains him.

| Instead of | Do this |
|---|---|
| Locking modules | Show all 3, always |
| Assigning them a module | Let them see where they are and click in |
| Asking permission questions | Onboarding fills in what they have, and the map updates |

**The home screen is the journey map, not a dashboard of features.** That single decision
resolves the gate, resolves the two-avatars-from-opposite-ends problem, and resolves the
retention risk, all at once.

**Instrumentation still does the selling.** Let someone practice on day 1 with a weak offer,
then show them their $/call. That sells module 1 harder than a lock ever could. **A lock
creates a refund. A number creates a purchase.**

## Where the free course sits

The 3 live classrooms do **not** currently map to the 3 stages:

| Live classroom | Lessons | Maps to |
|---|---|---|
| Start Here | 2 | neither, it is About Me |
| Building & Marketing | 7 (2 sections) | BUILD **and** MARKET fused |
| The Sales Framework | 9 | CLOSE |

Ryan describes 3 courses. There are 3 classrooms that are not those 3 courses. Reconciling
them is a later-phase job.

**MARKET is nearly empty.** Of roughly 20 catalogued assets, about 10 are close, 7 are build,
and only 2 are genuinely market. **Build module 2's content before module 2's software.**

And 1 lesson that looks like marketing sits in the sales classroom **on purpose**, because
THE LAW draws the module boundary: once you have leads, you have a sales problem. Reaching
out to a generated lead takes sales copy, so that teaching is sales, not marketing. MARKET
ends the moment the lead exists. Same reasoning: **a phone number is a lead, so cold calling
is a sales problem too**, which is why the cold call framework is CLOSE doctrine. MARKET's
whole job is producing the lead; the first word said to that lead is sales.
